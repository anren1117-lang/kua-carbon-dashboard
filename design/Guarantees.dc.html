Keeping it that way
The risk is the page added next year
Connecting the pages once was the easy half. A page written months from now can quietly go back to the old built-in number. Three things stop that.
01
The built-in figure is a first frame, not an answer
It exists so a page isn’t blank in the moment before the real data arrives. If a page is still showing it once the data has landed, that is a fault, not a fallback.
02
The site refuses to build if a page forgets
An automatic check reads every page before release. If one displays a kilowatt-hour or tonnes total without also reading one of the five live sources, the release stops and the page is named. This kind of mistake never shows itself otherwise — nothing breaks, the page simply disagrees with the rest of the site — which is why it needs a check rather than a careful reviewer.

03
Two exceptions, both written down
Emission rates. The kilograms of CO₂e per kilowatt-hour don’t change when the campus total does, so pages may use them directly. Checked from 400,000 to 2.5 million kilowatt-hours — either side of the campus’s 1.66 million.
Teaching material. Four sentences in the student lessons quote the headline figures and are written by hand. A second check fails if they drift away from the real totals.

Where this came from · 15 September 2026
The Scope 2 live dashboard had been left behind when the other pages moved onto live data. Its four tabs, Time Analysis among them, kept working from the built-in annual figure, so entering a month moved the rest of the site and not them. Its month grid decided which months counted as measured from a fixed list, so a newly entered month could never appear there at all.
Both are fixed. The check described in 02 was in place before the fix, and it flagged one further page that had been missed.
