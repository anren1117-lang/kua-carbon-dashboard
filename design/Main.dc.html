What happens to a number after someone types it in
Four things get entered through the admin portal. Every electricity figure on the site is then computed from them — the pages no longer carry totals typed in by hand. Fuel, travel and waste reach the site by their own routes and aren’t shown here. This is the electricity path, left to right.
Two words used throughout: the BMS is the building management system, the campus’s own network of meters. The reporting year is the twelve months the dashboard currently covers. Text in this colour names the underlying file or table, for anyone who wants to look it up.

2 · The rules, written once
There is one answer to each of these questions, and every page reads it.
How a month’s campus total is chosen
electricityLedger.js
If a master-meter month exists, it is used as it stands.
Otherwise, add up the individual building meters and multiply by a scale factor — between them they don’t cover the whole campus.
That factor comes from the months where both numbers exist, so it is measured rather than assumed.
If it falls outside 0.5–1.5, the month is withheld instead of published.
Whole months only
buildingMonths.js
A building’s month counts only if it covers the full calendar month and falls inside the reporting year. Half a month sitting beside a full one would look like a building that had halved its use.
Turning a short window into a year
parseBmsExport.js
A thirty-day export is scaled up by its own share of the year. January and July are not the same thirty days.
An export that falls outside the reporting year returns nothing at all. The alternative — treating it as a full year — would publish one month’s total as if it were twelve.

The rule every figure follows
Show the live value; fall back to the number built into the site only while the live one is still loading. That built-in number is what appears in the first instant a page opens — never what it settles on.
Provenance travels with the figure
Each number carries how it was arrived at, all the way to the page: measured, cited, estimated, or scaled where the building-meter rule above was used. A reader can always tell which one they are looking at.
